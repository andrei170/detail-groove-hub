Foundation

Detail Groove | Copy Strategy

Foundation doc - Voice, brand pillars, headline bank, framing for all AG1 copy. Prepared: 2026-05-16

Voice

Detailer-operator-turned-business-coach. The register is the guy who has had his own detailing rig, learned the hard way what kills a detailing business (underpriced jobs, no systems, no repeat-client machine), figured it out, and now teaches it. The voice is operator-to-operator. Not influencer. Not agency coach. Not tech-bro. The tone is "here is what is costing you on your next job" - not "unlock your potential."

Parallel to Keith Kalfas: Keith is trades-operator-to-trades-operator (landscaping). Detail Groove is the same register, same logic, applied to detailing. The foreman becomes the shop owner. The bid becomes the quote. The route becomes the client roster. The math is always the math.

Owned positioning phrase (use verbatim): "Zero to $20K/mo"

Voice Tokens

Use: - the quote, the job, the client, the route, the repeat client, the referral machine - your shop, your rig, your rate, your schedule - hitting $20K/mo, crossing $10K/mo, stuck at $5K/mo - detailing business (always "business" paired - never just "detailing" which reads as technique) - systems, SOPs, client acquisition - 10+ years hands-on - "zero to $20K/mo" - seat cap, cohort, live call, group call, weekly call - install, build, deploy (for what AG1 does)

Avoid (in addition to universal AG1 ban list): - technique-first language: swirl marks, clay bar, IDA certification, panel wipe, correction pass (this is the technician register, not the business-owner register) - "passive income" - Detail Groove's model requires active delivery - "financial freedom" without a specific dollar number - "scale your detailing brand" - it is a detailing BUSINESS - "community" as a standalone promise - it is what's inside the product, not the product - "grind" - overused in trades, reads generic - "blueprint" in body copy (fine as product name if they use it, not as AG1 copy adjective) - "next level" / "next-level" (on universal ban list)

Brand Pillars

  1. Business-first, not craft-first. The ICP is a detailer who wants to run a business, not a hobbyist who wants to detail better cars. Every headline and body paragraph addresses the business problem, not the craft problem. "How do I get more clients" not "how do I remove deeper scratches."

  2. Zero to $20K/mo is the owned benchmark. This is the brand's north star claim. All copy orbits it. Not "$100K/year" (too generic). Not "six figures" (too vague). "$20K/mo" is specific, verifiable, and the exact ceiling Detail Groove has publicly staked. Use it. Do not round it.

  3. Operator-to-operator authority. 10+ years in the field. The brand earned the right to coach because it ran the rig before it ran the classroom. Copy reflects that. "I have done this" not "I have studied this." Always ground credibility in time-in-trade, not certifications or methodology names.

  4. Systems solve what hustle cannot. The ICP is usually stuck not because they do not work hard but because they have no client acquisition system, no pricing model that protects margins, no SOPs that let them hire without losing quality. The copy frames systems as the exit from the hustle trap, not as extra complexity.

  5. Implementation over inspiration. The courses have content. The workshops have hands-on reps. The cohort has installs. Copy reflects what comes out the other side - the quote sheet, the booked client, the first hire - not how the operator "feels transformed." Numbers beat adjectives every time.

Headline Bank

For hub, landing pages, ad creative, and email subjects:

Hub / landing hero: - The business side of detailing. From your first job to $20K/mo. - 252K operators watching. The ones hitting $20K/mo stopped watching and started building. - You already know how to detail. Here is what's keeping your business stuck. - The quote that costs you the job. The client roster that never repeats. The shop stuck at $5K/mo. We have seen all three. - 10+ years of detailing. The first few were the expensive ones.

Cohort / mid-tier (for Phase 2 build when cohort exists): - The 8-week cohort for the detailer past YouTube and not yet at $20K/mo. - Client acquisition. Pricing for profitability. Your first hire. In that order. - 25 seats. Weekly group call. This is the install version of the podcast. - By week 4 your pricing is fixed. By week 8 your client roster runs without you chasing it.

Workshop: - 4 students. 2 days. Houston. The operational playbook, not another video. - $1,200 for the 2-day version of what most detailers take 3 years to figure out.

Email subject lines: - Why your detailing business is stuck at the same number two months in a row - The $20K/mo detailing business: what it looks like operationally - 3 things Detail Groove built differently (and what they cost us when we didn't have them) - Your next client is not coming from where you think

DM / outreach hook: - "Took a fresh pass at the funnel since August. Three things I'd build differently."

Framing for the Funnel (AG1 install)

  • Top of funnel (quiz, YouTube end-cards, podcast outro, IG hooks): business-problem specific. "You are quoting $150 for a full detail and wondering why you cannot cross $5K/mo" beats "grow your detailing business." The quiz surfaces which specific bottleneck is costing the most right now.

  • Mid funnel (cohort landing, VSL, waitlist): the cohort is the install version of the podcast. The podcast tells you what to look for. The cohort walks your specific quote, your specific client acquisition system, your specific first-hire plan.

  • High-ticket (coaching application, $20K-$25K/mo): the cohort graduate who wants ongoing 1:1 applies for coaching after completing the cohort. The high-ticket stays as premium continuation, not cold entry.

Voice Test

Every paragraph passes if a detailer doing $8K/mo with 1 helper, working 55 hours/week, and stuck at the same revenue number for 6 months nods reading it - not rolls their eyes. If the copy could appear in a generic "build your online business" course, rewrite it. If it includes a number, that number is operator-specific: not "thousands of dollars" - $150/job, $5K/mo plateau, $20K/mo ceiling.

The three-second test: Read the headline out loud as if Detail Groove were saying it to a detailer sitting in the parking lot of a job site. If a word would not get said in that context, cut it.
